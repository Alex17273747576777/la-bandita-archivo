## ⚡ On one page

Google is installing a developer census for Android. The program is called "Android developer verification" and it works like this: for an app to install on certified Android devices, its developer will have to be registered and verified — with a real identity, declared package names and linked signing keys.

Today is September 15. **15 days** remain until the first public milestone: on September 30, four countries and seven stores enter the filter. If you live in the Dominican Republic — or any country outside the pilot — your date is another one: 2027, when the filter reaches all apps on certified devices.

The voices sit in two camps that this guide quotes by name: Google, which sells it as accountability against malware; and F-Droid with the Keep Android Open campaign, who read it as a census that breaks the free distribution of apps. What everybody does have: a clock. The campaign keeps a public counter: 110 days checked on Sep 14 — the counter runs by itself.

## 🪪 What verification is (in Google's words)

The official definition fits in one sentence: "Android developer verification links real-world entities —individuals and organizations— with their Android apps."

Google's frame: it establishes accountability. It discourages malicious actors, makes it harder for whoever did harm to repeat under another name, and gives the user more confidence about who's behind an app. The registration fee gets compared in the FAQ itself to the Google store's historic $25 quota.

The opposing read (F-Droid, Keep Android Open, the letter's signers): that's not a malware scanner — it's a census. It doesn't analyze files: it analyzes people. And it puts the weight of registration on whoever distributes, not on whoever distributes badly. Both reads get quoted in full below; this guide doesn't pick a side for you.

## 🛒 The two consoles and the registration flow

There are two doors, depending on how you distribute:

- **Play Console** — if your apps live in Google's store. Registration integrates with what already existed.
- **Android Developer Console** — the new console, for everything else: alternative stores, direct distribution, organized sideload.

The new console's flow, per its official guide (opened today), in seven steps:

```
1. Create the account
2. Choose how you distribute your apps
3. Complete identity verification
   (requirements change by account type)
4. Register your apps' package names
5. Automate the flow with the new APIs
6. Resolve package-name duplicates
   (when two projects claim the same one)
7. Review
```

The delicate points the documentation confirms: registration requires declaring ALL the package names you distribute, and evidence that you control the signing keys. In other words: it's not a form — it's a complete inventory of what you've published.

## 🏪 The seven stores of September 30

The September 30 milestone doesn't hit "all stores": it hits the declared participants.

```
1.  Google Play
2.  Galaxy Store   (Samsung)
3.  GetApps        (Xiaomi)
4.  HONOR Store
5.  OPPO Store
6.  Palm Store
7.  V-Appstore     (vivo)
```

Four countries in the pilot: Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore and Thailand. Certified devices on Android 7 or above. If your app lives in those stores and countries, September 30 needs its developer verified. If you live outside the pilot, your calendar is 2027's — but the process can be done early, and Google recommends it.

What does NOT change on September 30: direct APK sideload (installing a file by hand), F-Droid and the alternative stores outside the list. That's the 2027 matter.

## 🎓 Limited distribution accounts: the side door

The month's novelty, confirmed today in three official sources (the FAQ, the dedicated guide and the June blog):

```
For whom:     students, teachers, hobbyists,
              whoever is learning
What it lets: register apps and share them with up to
              20 devices — which the end user
              authorizes explicitly
What it asks: no government ID, no fee
Its guide has: key dates, what the account can do,
              cost, how sharing works, whether it's
              for you, and what you need to register
```

It's a real window for the classroom, the workshop and the hobby. With a hard ceiling: twenty devices don't sustain a community project or an open distribution. It's the door to learn with, not the door to live in.

## 🛠️ The advanced flow (the 24-hour letter one)

For whoever can't or won't go through full registration, there's an advanced flow. Per the campaign's documentation (also verified last week), it works like this:

- Nine steps, described in the guide.
- A wait of roughly 24 hours.
- It runs through Play Services — Google's service, not the open operating system.

That last point is the one generating the most discussion: the alternative path goes through the same proprietary component as the standard one. If Play Services isn't there — phones without Google — the flow doesn't apply.

The official FAQ keeps its "Advanced flow" section as a recognized route.

## 📜 The open letter: the three objections

Keep Android Open organized an open letter to Google (read today on its page). Its structure is three blocks:

**1. Our concerns.** Identity-based registration turns software distribution into an identified act before a corporation; the impact falls on small developers, projects anonymous for safety and communities outside the formal circuits.

**2. The existing measures are enough.** Play Protect already scans; stores already filter; the system's security mechanisms already exist. The census adds no scanner: it adds a registry of people.

**3. The request.** That Google withdraw the identity-registration requirement as a condition of distribution.

The figures the letter itself shows: 71 signers, 23 countries. Among the visible ones: AdGuard, Aurora Store, BEUC, Brave, Calyx, CCC, Codeberg, F-Droid, FUTO, GrapheneOS, IzzyOnDroid, KDE, microG, Nextcloud, Obtainium, Tor, Vivaldi and the Karisma Foundation, among others. Being on the list isn't a recommendation to install anything — it's the map of who signed.

## 📣 The campaign and its clock

https://keepandroidopen.org

The campaign kept its public counter on the front page: **110 days** (checked Sep 14) — toward what its FAQ calls the lockdown, placing it around January 2027. Its front-page sentence: "Your phone is about to stop being yours."

What the campaign documents about standard registration: a fee, government ID, evidence of holding the signing key, and the declaration of all package names. Its slogan — "Do not sign up" — belongs to the campaign; this guide quotes it, doesn't dictate it.

Its FAQ also settles the question the community repeats most: September does NOT include F-Droid; the date the filter reaches F-Droid isn't published. The campaign's counter is the only public countdown that exists on 2027 — that's why this guide quotes it with its date.

## 📗 The opposing read: F-Droid

https://f-droid.org/en/2025/09/29/google-developer-registration-decree.html

F-Droid's analysis (published September 29, 2025, opened today) titles the program "Google's developer registration decree" and takes it apart in four sections:

- **The move to break free distribution** — the thesis: no identity, no distribution; no distribution, no free software on Android.
- **The security lure** — malware is already fought with scanning; the census doesn't analyze files, it analyzes authors.
- **The right to run** — your phone, your software: the bottom line the letter defends.
- **What they propose** — alternatives that don't go through identity registration.

F-Droid also keeps its "under threat" banner across its site, the campaign active. For the free ecosystem, this is THE topic of the year.

## ❓ FAQ

**Does September 30 block me from installing APKs by hand?**
No. That milestone hits the participating stores in four countries. Direct sideload and F-Droid stay out — for now. Your serious clock is 2027.

**Do I have to do anything TODAY?**
If you live in the pilot or publish in those stores: yes, verify. If not: no — but getting to know the process before it's your turn is wise.

**Does verification cost?**
The registration gets compared in the FAQ to Play's $25. Limited distribution accounts pay no fee and ask for no ID.

**What if I only make apps for myself?**
Twenty devices is the limited accounts' world: no ID, no fee. For an intimate circle, it's enough.

**Does this kill F-Droid in September?**
No — and it isn't published when, if it happens. It's the most repeated question and the honest answer is: nobody outside Google has that date.

**What's this about "certified devices"?**
The ones leaving the factory with Google's services. A phone without certification lives outside the filter's reach — with everything else that implies.

**Is the 110-day counter official?**
It's the campaign's, not Google's. Its date — the lockdown around January 2027 — is its reading of the calendar. Google says "2027" flat. Two clocks, two voices.

**Where do I read the sources without anyone's spin?**
In the links below: hub, guides, FAQ and blogs are Google's; the letter and FAQ are the campaign's; the analysis is F-Droid's. Everything opens today.
