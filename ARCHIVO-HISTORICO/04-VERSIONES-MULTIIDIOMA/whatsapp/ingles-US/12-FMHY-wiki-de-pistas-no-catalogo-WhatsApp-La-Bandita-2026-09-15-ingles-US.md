## ⚡ On one page

FMHY — "freemediaheckyeah" — introduces itself as "the largest collection of free stuff on the internet." It's an enormous community wiki, alive today, with a precise role on the knowledge map: **it's a first-rate discoverer and a verifier of nothing.**

The difference matters because April proved it with damage: somebody emptied a slice of the wiki into a 340-thousand-character thread and sold it as a verified catalog. It wasn't. Every FMHY lead is a first link — opening the project's house, reading its license, checking its version and owner is the work nobody can do for you, neither this wiki nor this guide.

Today's state, September 14: the site responds with its September monthly post active, its beginners' guide loads, and the repo generating it counts ★14,592 — with the peculiarity that its git code hasn't pushed since May, and the wiki isn't dead for that. Both things, counted below.

## 🌐 What FMHY is and how it works

https://fmhy.net

FMHY is a wiki maintained by its community: thousands of entries of tools, sites and resources organized by categories — privacy, AI, video, audio, gaming, reading, downloading, torrenting, educational, mobile, linux-macos, non-english, misc, among others. Its mechanics:

- The community proposes and curates entries continuously.
- A changelog records what changed — and its rhythm is visible: the wiki publishes **monthly news posts** (September 2026's is online today).
- There's an SFW variant (no adult content) for whoever prefers it.
- The project's culture is that of an index: describe, link, guarantee nothing.

Its size is its virtue and its limit: it covers everything, audits nothing. It's the world's largest library in its genre — and like any big library, the value isn't in having it all: it's in knowing how to use the catalog.

## 🔒 The Base64 interstitial (what it is and isn't)

On entering, FMHY sometimes shows a notice about links encoded in Base64. What's behind it, no detours:

```
It is:    a design measure. Some of the wiki's links
          get published encoded so automatic
          crawlers don't index them.
          The site itself offers an on-page decoder.

It is not: a security system, a mandatory rite,
           or an instruction from this guide. The user
           decides whether to use that route; the decoder
           belongs to the site and runs in your browser.

And it is not: a reason to copy encoded links into
           a post. Links go in the clear here or they don't go.
```

The wiki does its engineering to survive the indexers; this guide family does its own to not paste recipes. Each protects its own.

## 📦 The repo behind the wiki

The curiosity that teaches something: the git has been quiet for months and the wiki is alive — re-verified TODAY, Sep 15: it loads with its September post. How? The wiki is served through its own routes (its web and its deployments) and the public repo doesn't reflect every move. The general lesson you already saw in this family: **a quiet git ≠ a dead project; a living project ≠ everything verified.** A repo is a layer, not the whole project.

## 🧭 Discovery ≠ verification: the complete funnel

The house's rule fits in one line — finding isn't verifying. Unfolded, the funnel turning a lead into something citable:

```
1. DISCOVERY
   FMHY (or a group, or a friend) says:
   "this app is good."
   ── this is worth exactly zero as verification.

2. OPENING
   The project's house gets opened — its repo or site —
   THAT DAY. Not the group's screenshot: the house.

3. READING
   Who's the owner? How long has it existed?
   What license does it declare? When was its last
   version and its last push? Is it archived?

4. CROSS-CHECKING
   Do other independent sources name it?
   Does its community answer issues?
   Are its signature/checksums public?

5. REAL USE
   Does somebody of flesh and bone use it and can
   tell how it goes? Real use is the last
   door — and the only one nobody can open for you.

6. LISTING
   Only then: a listing with a date, in a
   guide like this. With an expiration date at that.
```

Any shortcut skipping steps — "it's on FMHY, period," "they recommend it on Telegram, period" — is the exact gesture that made April's thread a 340-thousand-character encumbrance.

## 🔬 A real example, end to end

So the funnel doesn't stay theory, the route this family already walked with a real entry:

```
1. The wiki (and half the internet) mentions "Mihon"
   as a manga reader.

2. The house gets opened: github.com/mihonapp/mihon

3. It gets read: 23,575 stars · Apache-2.0 ·
   version 0.20.4 from August · TODAY's push ·
   the mihonapp organization with history since 2024.

4. It gets cross-checked: Keiyoushi (the extensions
   warehouse) lists it first among supported apps;
   its issues have activity; the ecosystem's community
   names it Tachiyomi's de facto successor.

5. Real use: the folks in the band who use it,
   with their opinion.

6. Listing: today Mihon has its own guide inside
   this family (the reader-forks one), with its
   cutoff date.
```

Six steps. Everything needed for "Mihon" to go from lead to information. And everything that did NOT happen the day somebody pasted a whole wiki into a thread.

## ❓ FAQ

**Can I use FMHY?**
You can read it: it's public and its role as an index is legitimate. What this guide doesn't do is send you to its destinations or empty it here. Every click inside it is a discovery — verification goes separately, with the funnel above.

**Is it safer than googling?**
Not asserted. It's another list, curated by other people, with other incentives. Search engines send you to paid ads; wikis, to what the community noted. Neither door verifies for you.

**Does its Android section replace this family's stores guide?**
No. The stores guide opened each project on cutoff day; a wiki section is a list with another date and another criterion.

**And its music section?**
Same answer: the family has a music guide with verified listings. The wiki may point at the same things — or at things nobody opened yet.

**The repo says May but the wiki is alive — which is the truth?**
Both: the wiki is served through its own deployments and the repo doesn't reflect every change. A quiet git isn't a dead project — the repo lesson already said it.

**Is Base64 piracy?**
It's text encoding — the same technique your phone uses to send images by mail. What each person does with the links is their business and their law. Nothing gets decoded for anybody here.

**So what is FMHY good for, in the end?**
For what its definition said: the ecosystem's largest index. As a map of what EXISTS, it has no rival. As proof of what works and is safe, it's worth nothing — and it doesn't pretend to.
