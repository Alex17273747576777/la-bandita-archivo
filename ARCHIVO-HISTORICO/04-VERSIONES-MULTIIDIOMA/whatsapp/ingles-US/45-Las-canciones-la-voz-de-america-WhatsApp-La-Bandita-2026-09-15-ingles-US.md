## ⚡ The songs, on one page

The Library is also sung. This guide joins two southern houses that call for each other: **Los de Las Condes** — the Chilean Neofolclor quintet that turned a discarded song into an anthem — and **Mercedes Sosa**, the Negra of Tucumán, whose catalog lives open on the net with 411 songs and almost forty million reads. The house's rule for listing songs: **first the verified lyric, then the name** — and always separating the author from the interpreter, because in Latin American song the voice belongs to one and the work to another. No lyrics pasted here: every title goes with its door to read it where it lives.

## 🇨🇱 El corralero and his quintet (Chile)

The main listing is on Musicapopular.cl, the Chilean encyclopedia (https://www.musicapopular.cl/grupo/los-de-las-condes/): a quintet of the Neofolclor movement that reactivated the tonada in the 60s. The story worth the price: Sergio Sauvalle composed **"El corralero"** — the tonada of the peon facing his horse's sacrifice — and **Los Huasos Quincheros rejected it**. Pedro Messone heard it, saw its worth, and with Sauvalle, Pepe Hernández and Renato Ledermann he built Los de Las Condes. At the 1965 Viña Festival the song lost to Los Cuatro Cuartos' "Mano nortina" — and that same night René *Chino* Urquidi told them the line time would sign: **"You didn't win, but it will be your song that endures."**

- **"El corralero"** (Sergio Sauvalle) — the Chilean song that endured, from the LP *Los de Las Condes* (1966).
- **"La sequía"** and **"La mula rosilla"** — the other widely spread titles.
- **The 1966 LP, complete** (13 tracks): Tonada del Viejo Amor (Falú/Dávalos) — La Burrerita (Sofanor Tobar) · La Llorona — El Pescador · La Tropillita — **Canción de Cuna Negra (Rolando Alarcón)** · El Corralero — El Alazán · Yo Defiendo a Mi Tierra — Guantanamera · El Barco Fantasma — Sirilla de la Candelaria · La Burriquita.
- **The brief and whole discography:** 4 LPs — *Los de Las Condes* (1966) · *Los de Las Condes internacionales* (1967) · *Los muchachos se divierten* (1968) · *Como ésta no habrá otra* (1968, the party recorded in studio, 26 songs) — plus 6 RCA singles. Dissolved around 1970; Messone and Sauvalle went on solo, always with central-zone folklore.

## ✍️ The authors behind the voice

The listing's lesson: the Negra almost never signs what she sings — and that's why she's everybody's voice. Numhauser, Parra, Walsh, Tejada Gómez, Isella, Luna, Ramírez, Gieco, Guarany, Yupanqui, Castilla, Leguizamón, Silvio: **thirteen authors from several countries in fifteen songs** — the Latin American song is itself a library of neighbors. Exact attribution, always: whoever wants the work should know who it's owed to.

The house had eight songs under observation without a link. The link closes the case; the verified lyric, the listing.

## ❓ Songs questions

**Why no lyrics in the guide?**
Because the lyric lives at its house and the house charges fair: here go the doors, the map and the authors. The guide that pastes somebody else's lyric steals twice — from the author and from the reader.

**Chile and Argentina together?**
They were always together: Falú and Dávalos on the Chilean LP of 1966, Violeta in the Tucumán voice. El corralero and the Negra are the same song on two shores.

## The songs' meme

> —Didn't another act win this song at Viña?
> —It lost in 1965. Today it's heritage.
> —And the Negra, how many of these did she write?
> —Almost none. She sang them all as if they were hers — which is the only honest way to own something: lending it your voice. The jury chose a winner; time chose something else.
