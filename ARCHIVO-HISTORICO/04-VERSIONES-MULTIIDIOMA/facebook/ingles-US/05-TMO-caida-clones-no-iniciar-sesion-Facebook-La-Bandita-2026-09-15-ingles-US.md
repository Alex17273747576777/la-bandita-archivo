## ⚡ On one page

TMO — TuMangaOnline, also known as ZonaTMO — was the largest Spanish-language pirate manga library of its kind. In March 2026 it stopped responding. On April 22, Spain's National Police reported the dismantling of the operation in Almería: three arrests, more than four million euros made from advertising, cryptocurrencies hidden inside a wall thermometer.

That's not group rumor. It's an official ministry note, picked up by international press, with the statement of the Korean rights holders who pushed the case.

What came after is the usual pattern: pages that look alike, that say "we're back," that ask for your email and password "to restore your list." That's not TMO. It's an awning with the stolen sign.

This piece exists for one single thing: that nobody of La Bandita's hands their account, their card or their phone to a sign. It doesn't exist to tell you where "to keep reading the same thing" — that would be facilitating the same thing that fell.

## 📜 What TMO was (no romance)

The names the operation used, per the case documents (they aren't destinations — the main one is down):

- TuMangaOnline — tumangaonline.com
- ZonaTMO — zonatmo.com
- In the network international press describes: also Visortmo, among others
- As affiliate sites of the same operation: Lectormanga.com and Tmofans.com

What the documents say: it operated since 2014, with unauthorized free access to a massive volume of works. Its monetization was pop-up advertising — described by police as mostly oriented to pornography — and the official communiqué itself notes many of its users were minors.

Behind it was a formal company: Nakamas Web SL, of Almería, already linked to the operation by earlier industry investigations. The legal web business ran in parallel to the illegal one — the case shows both faces of the same counter.

## 🚔 What Spain's National Police says

The content of the Interior Ministry's official note (April 22, 2026), unadorned:

- Dismantled an online service of illegal Spanish-language manga distribution, active since 2014, with millions of users.
- Profits exceeding €4,000,000, "generated exclusively through the insertion of advertising."
- Advertising "oriented to pornography"; "many of the users were minors."
- Three people arrested in Almería, as alleged perpetrators of a continuing offense against intellectual property.
- House search: seizure of the technological setup; a complementary page under development was thwarted.
- Two USB drives hidden in a wall thermometer: cold crypto wallets worth over €400,000.
- The police investigation had started in June 2025.

Two context notes the press added: the site's traffic in its last month was measured in tens of millions of visits, and the three arrested were released after the judicial handover — the criminal process kept going. A police note isn't a sentence; it's the declared facts of an operation.

## 🌏 What the Korean rights holders say

The COA association (Copyright Overseas Promotion Association) — grouping Korea's content giants — published its version on April 27, 2026, and it's still alive today:

- The shutdown of TuMangaOnline (formerly zonatmo.com) and its affiliate sites was achieved with support from Korea's Ministry of Culture and the industry's companies: Kakao Entertainment, Naver Webtoon, Lezhin, RIDI, Kidari Studio, Toomics and Topco Media.
- The case is presented as the first Korean webtoon enforcement abroad under their local law.
- The criminal trials in Spain were, at their date, "about to begin."

International press's version matches: the Korean rights holders, through their legal representation in Spain, gathered the evidence and put it in the authorities' hands. The company list above explains the weight of the push: when the owners of the planet's most-read content coordinate, the case doesn't die in a notice.

## 🔢 The numbers that don't match (and it's fine to say so)

Three sources, three traffic numbers. The most dramatic one doesn't get picked:

```
Spanish press (April 2026, citing Similarweb):
  33 million visits in March 2026.

The Korean association (Apr 27, 2026):
  ~86 million visits — but in March 2025.

Other outlets (blocked for verification):
  over 50 million in March 2026. Not used.
```

33 and 86 can't be merged: different months, or different methods, or both. Whoever cites "TMO had X visits" without saying whose X it is, is mixing. This guide doesn't assert one number: it asserts it was enormous — and that every figure carries its owner and its month.

## 🪞 The clones: they get named, not linked

The name serves to recognize the bait. The link would serve to fall.

What the press wrote in April: though the main site was cut off, other operations — with copied names one letter apart — stayed active. What the community reported that month: domains copying the name (zona-tmo, lectortmo, lectormangaa...), flagged as fraudulent clones, with imitated design and registration forms.

What this guide doesn't do, today or ever:

- It doesn't open those sites.
- It doesn't check whether "they're still up."
- It doesn't paste addresses.
- It doesn't build a list of "TMO-likes that are worth it."

Because a clone asking for the same email and the same password as old TMO isn't restoring anything: it's harvesting credentials.

## 🧭 How to read a domain without visiting it

You don't need to open the bait to read its face:

```
1. The TLD is not a quality seal.
   That .com fell doesn't turn .org, .vip,
   .app or .to into "the same TMO." Other contracts,
   other owners, another registry.

2. One extra letter is a trade.
   lectormanga vs lectormangaa: the typo IS the product.
   Imitators live off the finger's error.

3. "zona" + "tmo" in any order is not a brand
   the search engine will protect for you.
   The top ad was paid by whoever wants your click.

4. The padlock only says there's encryption.
   Banks have it, and so does phishing.
   It identifies no owners.

5. If "checking" requires typing your user
   and password, you're not checking anymore:
   you're handing it over.
```

## 🚩 Impostor signals

No single signal is enough. Several together, you parasitize:

- The domain isn't the one you used in 2025 — another ending, a typo, a new dash.
- "Recover your account": your email "doesn't exist" and they invite you to register again, your old password as a tip.
- The same old staff nicks, copied — the community already warned moderator names get usurped.
- Pop-ups to gambling, crypto, porn or APKs: the model police described of the original, without the original.
- They offer "support" over Telegram or WhatsApp, or a group selling "the rescued database."
- Search ads on top of the corpse: whoever pays to appear when TMO dies isn't the police.

A site passing one signal doesn't mean it's good. Passing three, you know what it is.

## 🔐 Your lists don't "recover"

This hurts, and it's the most effective bait, so it gets said plainly:

TMO kept your lists, your comments and your progress on THEIR servers. Those servers were seized. There's no honest "export my list from beyond" button — what there is: forms saying "reset," messages saying "your email isn't here, create an account," and sellers of "rescued databases." None of those three was ever verified because that kind of page doesn't get visited: they're described as the kind of bait, not as concrete sites.

What does exist going forward: routes with a known owner, where your account depends on the official service and your list is yours because you export it. Below, two examples opened today. They aren't TMO, they don't pretend to be, and they won't give back TMO's list. Healing the move starts with not handing your passport at the door.

## 📱 Readers and extensions: don't mix layers

The old text circulating in the groups mixed two different things, so they get separated:

```
The app (the reader)    Mihon, Kototoro, Tadami...
The source (the parser) the layer that talks to each site
The index (the recipe)  the list that bulk-installs sources
```

A free reader isn't a manga warehouse: it's an application. And pasting a "TMO" index address into the app is exactly the recipe this guide family doesn't copy — the reading destination is everyone's own decision, with their laws and their judgment.

If a "TMO" extension shows up in a warehouse, this guide doesn't recommend it and doesn't confirm it points at the original — because the original isn't there. The cited readers' versions were verified today: Kototoro published v2.1.2 this very morning, Tadami is on v0.62 of September 12.

## ❓ FAQ

**Does TMO ever come back?**
The brand is orphaned and the operators are out awaiting trial. Any "return" you see is signed by someone who isn't who fell. The awning, you already know what it is.

**Did my TMO data show up in the "rescued base"?**
If somebody sells you a database, they're selling other people's credentials — possibly yours. If you reused that password, change it wherever you reused it. That one you can do today.

**Is it true people only read, with no accounts?**
Police describing pornographic advertising and underage users gives the real context of "just reading." And the whole case is the lesson of who owns the risk on an ownerless route.

**Are free readers illegal then?**
The tool isn't: the app is a reader. The reading destination is everyone's decision. This guide doesn't recommend pirate reading destinations — not when TMO existed, not now.

**Where do I warn someone who fell for a clone?**
Change reused passwords, review the email's active sessions, and tell them exactly this: the padlock identifies no owners, and "Telegram support" isn't support.

**What if the clone "really works"?**
Fake ATMs hand out bills too. The business is in what they collect along the way.
